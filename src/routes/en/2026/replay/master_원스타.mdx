## 2.4 star: The abcd-priority-agent Model

A phased strategy model organized in the order A > B > C > D

### 2.4.1 The Problem

Rushes of $3$–$5$ warriors became popular: they target the early game, when the starting $500$ gold has to go into base construction, by instead spending the $500$ gold on warriors and attacking HQ.

Early on it was advantageous to send $2$ warriors to strongholds to claim bases first. Because of that, HQ often had only $1$ warrior, and was vulnerable to $3$–$5$-warrior rushes.

#### 2.4.1.1 Attempts at a Solution

We tried recalling the warriors sent out to expand when enemy warriors came near HQ. Depending on the distances between the map's strongholds, the warriors often returned too late and we lost,
or we started out at a labor disadvantage.

For a $5$-warrior rush, they'd spent their early $500$ gold, so if we could reliably hold, we won. For $3$–$5$-warrior rushes, defending at HQ with $(\text{attackers} - 1)$ was also possible.

The cause of losing to $3$–$5$-warrior rushes was the warrior deficit from the gold and personnel spent on base construction. The gold and personnel spent building the $2$nd base in particular were fatal.
So we sent only $1$ warrior to build the $1$st base and had it watch the opponent's behavior.

If the opponent also built their $1$st base, they spent gold and labor, so it was hard for them to mass warriors. And even if they massed warriors after that,
our per-turn income had grown, making the rush easier to hold.

#### 2.4.1.2 What Was Implemented

**Phase A**

Send only $1$ warrior to the nearest stronghold and build the first base. Once the enemy has built their first base, move to B. When enemy warriors are within $2$ areas of HQ, train so that
$(\text{attackers within 2 areas} - 1)$ are at HQ. If HQ has no labor, train to fill the labor slots.

**Phase B**

After confirming the enemy's first construction, start building the $2$nd base. Move to C.

#### 2.4.2 Mid-Game Structure (C–D)

At game start, count the empty strongholds on the battlefield excluding the central stronghold.
Set $\text{half the empty strongholds excluding the center} - 1 = \text{optimal base count}$.

**Phase C (establishing a hub)**

The base located at the center of our HQ/bases is designated the "hub".

All trained warriors other than labor gather at the hub.

Harassment and defense are carried out around the hub's troops.

If $\text{our bases} > \text{optimal base count}$, move to Phase D.

Warrior training by optimal base count
- Compare against the enemy's total warriors, excluding both sides' labor and expansion personnel.
- $\text{optimal base count} > \text{our bases}$: train warriors to match the enemy's troops.
- $\text{our bases} \ge \text{optimal base count}$: train $4$ more warriors than the enemy.

Construction limits by optimal base count
- $\text{our bases} \ge \text{optimal base count}$: stop base construction.
- $\text{optimal base count} > \text{enemy bases}$: start base construction.

Attack timing
- $\text{enemy bases} > \text{our bases}$: attack, harassing so they can't gain a stronghold advantage.
- Excluding $\text{our bases} = \text{optimal bases}$ && $\text{our bases} > \text{enemy bases}$
- Harass if we have $4$ or more warriors than the enemy, excluding both sides' labor and expansion personnel.


**Phase D (reinforcing staying power)**

Upgrade HQ when its level is equal to or lower than the enemy's.

When HQ's level is higher than the enemy's, or all upgrades are done,
- Priority $1$: upgrade the central-stronghold hub first.
- Priority $2$: upgrade the places most often passed through when moving from HQ.
- Priority $3$: upgrade bases within 3 areas of the hub.

### 2.4.3 Ideas

<div id="relay" />

#### 2.4.3.1 Relay (Boosting) System

When moving, if the route passes through one of our bases on the way to the destination, move the warrior to that intermediate base instead of the original destination.
When the sent warrior enters the intermediate base, move the warrior that was already there on to the original destination.

This way personnel leave and arrive in the same turn, preserving labor while moving $1$ turn faster.

#### 2.4.3.2 HQ Relay (Boosting) System
On a turn when HQ is about to train troops, if there is an order for the trained troops, an HQ laborer acts instead.
The troops produced at HQ then fill that labor slot.

#### 2.4.3.3 Observation-Waypoint Defense System
When the enemy has $2$ adjacent attack candidates and reinforcing after the attack path is confirmed would be too late, move the reinforcements to an intermediate "observation waypoint" off the enemy's route.
Once the enemy's actual attack direction is confirmed on the next turn, reinforce that base.

#### 2.4.3.4 Troop Preservation System
When there are no reinforcements against an enemy attack, check whether the base would be destroyed within $2$ turns. If destruction within $2$ turns is certain,
on the turn the base is attacked, move all of its troops to the hub to preserve them.

<div id="escort" />

#### 2.4.3.5 Escort System

1. A large enemy force of $8$ or more is confirmed to be attacking our HQ directly
2. An enemy base is built within $2$ areas of HQ / an enemy stronghold is built on the path along which troops are sent to the hub
3. Enemy troops are within $3$ areas of HQ and defeat is expected

Reserve troops are stockpiled at HQ. These troops are called the "escort".

They reinforce our strongholds near HQ / assault enemy strongholds. They are released once the threat is gone.
