First, each of us decided to quickly build and submit code with whatever tactics we wanted.

### ⭐byeol

I thought simply: **build lots of bases**. Crossing the center line lets the opponent reach a base faster, so we too build starting from the strongholds closest to our HQ. Battles $7$ and $8$ use about half the strongholds, so for now the goal was to build up to half. Then, with enough gold, upgrade, produce warriors and strike the enemy HQ.

Problem (battle $2$) – For this kind of growth strategy, an **HQ rush** like battle $2$ was the most lethal. I came up with the following solutions and asked Claude Opus 4.8 which was appropriate.

- Defend at the base on the chokepoint
- Set an approach criterion and, when the enemy approaches the HQ, return to defend it
- Leave $2$ warriors at the HQ from the start

&rarr; <strong>On approach to the HQ, "produce extra warriors at the HQ to block."</strong>

If within $8$ hops of the HQ, pin the HQ's existing warriors in defense so they do not leave. In an emergency, produce warriors and recall those that went out. Added HQ level-up logic and more.

Problem (battle $3$) – Judged the threat too early, so too many forces stayed at the HQ and the HQ was destroyed.

&rarr; Realized hop distance does not fit, since map size varies. Narrowed the condition: the criterion became **the number of enemies that crossed the center line onto our side**.

Problem – Now we build more bases than the sample AIs and gold income is fine. But the opponent sends $10$+ at once to wipe out bases, while my code sends too few.

&rarr; Raise the attack headcount, and gather at a rally point near the enemy HQ before sending. Built a condition that accounts for the enemy HQ's defense and HQ level.

Problem – With no upgrade logic, the existing attack condition was never met in battle $5$.

&rarr; Wrote upgrade logic. Keep a set amount for producing warriors. With few bases, upgrade bases; with many, prioritize HQ level-ups.

Many attempts followed at $7$ wins.

Problem (battles $7$, $8$) – We only keep losing strongholds.

&rarr; Attack bases weakest-first, considering enemy base level and warrior count. We also have to defend when our base is attacked.

&rarr; Added base-defense logic, which mistook an HQ rush for a base attack and the HQ was destroyed. Removed the base-defense logic in the end.

For the tiebreak, when the game reaches the very late stage and our level is below the enemy HQ's, prioritize the HQ upgrade.

If the enemy masses $6$+ warriors and outnumbers us, produce warriors too.

When our HQ is safe and the enemy sends forces our way, leaving their HQ weakly defended, attack their HQ through the gap.

## 2. #69251

## 3. #84910

Problem: When the enemy team brought a large army in the late game, our attacking warriors came back too late.

&rarr; During a late enemy rush, returning to the HQ takes priority for anyone closer than the midpoint. That is, when a large enemy force is coming, HQ defense is the top priority for everyone except warriors closer to the enemy's bases on the current map and the fixed laborers.

## 4. #86205

&rarr; After turn $120$, if the attack force is large enough and no empty bases remain, split into two to capture enemy bases

## 5. #88363

## 6. #95191

Problem: We could not defend against early rushes. An early rush builds few bases, masses warriors, hits the opponent's bases fast to stop them earning money, and wins on the early production gap. In the Pretests we lost to most teams using this tactic.

&rarr; Tried to implement an early rush ourselves (**hyeon09**)

Basic premise: build two bases.

1. Attack with the initial $3$ as much as they can<br />
   &rarr; On larger maps travel is slow, so bases got built faster than they could be destroyed; $3$ could destroy at most $2$ bases.
2. Attack with the initial $4$ as much as they can, and keep destroying bases with reinforcements until turn $80$<br />
   &rarr; Reinforcements meant little. They arrived a step behind the initial squad of $4$ and were no match even for $\text{base }1 + \text{warrior }1$. They died too fast.
3. No reinforcements. Keep hitting bases in squads of $4$ until turn $80$ (keep forming squads of $4$)<br />
   &rarr; Then it felt like it took too long to build new bases. Wouldn't building bases in that time be more effective?
4. Keep sending squads of $4$ at enemy bases until turn $60$<br />
   &rarr; The best result of the above. Still, $7.5$ wins at most in the AI battles.<br />
   &rarr; Patching further, the AI battles dropped to $2$ wins. Judging further patching pointless, we switched to the code with early-rush defense logic.

We used the code that had been our biggest weakness, implemented in reverse, to shore up our defense. This part gave us our biggest jump in rank. Had time allowed, it is a strategy we would have liked to keep using. (hooray **hyeon09**~~) 👏

## 7. Early-rush defense code (#96716) &rarr; patched code (#97352)

Rush-response idea (**mjyuung**): over-expanding bases early prevents producing the warriors to respond. What if we build only $\text{enemy base count} + 1$?

&rarr; Early rushes, apart from unusual cases, could now be blocked well enough. Confirmed in local battles against hyeon09's rush code.

Code problem: Used base warriors first without using the surplus warriors inside the HQ

- &rarr; Changed to use, in order, $1$st the HQ's surplus, $2$nd the surplus outside, $3$rd base warriors.
- &rarr; For late-game attacks we also tried going in groups of $3$ rather than $1$ at a time, but the Pretest results came out low, and we learned that attacking in bunches is not good.
