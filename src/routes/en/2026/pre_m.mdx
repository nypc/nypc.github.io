## Rules

### 1 | Introduction

The game is played on the **battlefield**. The battlefield consists of $N$ areas indexed from $0$ through $N-1$, point-symmetric with respect to the origin. The leftmost area #$0$ and the rightmost area #$N-1$ each have a player's **headquarter**. Among areas without headquarter, $K$ areas are **stronghold**s, where **base**s can be built.

$N$, the size of battlefield, satisfies $51 \le N \le 109$, and $K$, the number of strongholds, satisfies $\lceil 0.15N \rceil \le K \le \lfloor 0.2N \rfloor$. $N$ and $K$ are both odd numbers.

Each player starts the game with $3$ **warriors** on their headquarter and $500$ gold. The headquarter is initially at level $1$, HP $10$, and the warriors are initially at HP $4$. If the headquarter is destroyed, the owner loses.

Every day, a player can deliver the following commands as many times as they want:

- **Upgrade**: A player can upgrade existing allied buildings (headquarter or base), or build a new base on a stronghold with no building.
  - To deliver an Upgrade command, there should be one or more allied warriors and no enemy warriors at the target area.
- **Move**: A player can command a warrior that is not currently in the moving state to move to a desired area.
  - However, if there is an allied building on the desired area, no gold is consumed.
- **Train**: By using $120 \times n$ gold, train $n$ warriors on the headquarter. $n$ is a non-negative integer less than or equal to `# of trainable warriors`.
  - You cannot use Train command more than once on the same day.

After delivering the commands, the day starts. The day consists of **The Morning**, **The Daytime**, and **The Evening**.

#### The Morning

The Morning consists of **Building Step** → **Moving Step** → **Training Step**, in order.

- In the **Building Step**, new bases are raised and buildings already built are upgraded by spending gold.
- In the **Moving Step**, the warriors that received Move commands on the same day or were already in moving state move up to one area according to the **Movement Rule** (Section 3).
  - You spend $10$ golds for every warrior that recieved the move command on the current day. However you do not pay if the destination have allied building.
- In the **Training Step**, train the commanded number of warriors and spend $120$ gold for each warrior.
  - The HP of each warrior is determined by the level of the headquarter.
  - The warriors are indexed uniquely, and warriors trained earlier have smaller indices.

#### The Daytime

**Battle Step** occurs.

- In the **Battle Step**, warriors attack other warriors or buildings with respect to the **Battle Rule** (Section 3).

#### The Evening

The Evening consists of **Labor Step** → **Supply Step**, in order.

- In the **Labor Step**, each building produces $15 \times x$ gold, where $x$ is the minimum value of `# of allied warriors in the area` and `# of labor slots`.
- In the **Supply Step**, each warrior consumes $2$ gold in increasing order of number. If there is not enough gold when a warrior tries to consume gold, the warrior loses 1 HP. If the HP of the warrior becomes 0, the warrior retreats, leaving the battlefield.

### 2 | Building

Buildings are the source of income.

After raising, upgrading or repairing buildings, the HP becomes its maximum HP.

At the start of each battle step, the turret in the building performs attacks in the same area as many times as its attack damage (AD). For further information, please refer to the Battle Step.

For each area, an Upgrade command can be delivered at most once per day. A building cannot be upgraded twice on the same day, nor can a building be upgraded on the same day it is built.

### 3 | Movement and Battle

#### Movement Rule

If there is no enemy warrior in the same area as a warrior that is performing a Move command, it moves by one area along a path whose sum of straight line distances between adjacent areas is minimum. Technical details are as follows:

<Details summary="Details">

Define **straight line distance** between $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ as $\left \lceil \sqrt{(x_1 - x_2) ^2 + (y_1 - y_2)^2 } \right \rceil$, i.e., the ceiling of Euclidean distance. Each warrior finds a path to its target by moving only through adjacent areas, minimizing the sum of straight line distances between consecutive areas on the path, and moves to the next area on that path. If multiple such paths exist, it moves to the area with the smaller index.

</Details>

After delivering a Move command to a warrior, it enters the moving state and cannot receive another Move command before it arrives at the target area. The moving state ends as soon as it arrives at the target area.

- After delivering a Move command that costs gold, the gold is not refunded even if the building is built on the target area.
- After delivering a Move command that does not cost gold, the movement is not aborted even if the building is destroyed on that area.

#### Battle Rule

1. In each area with a building, the building performs attacks as many times as its turret AD.
2. In each area, say the numbers of my warriors and the opponent's warriors are $a$ and $b$; the opponent is attacked $a$ times and I am attacked $b$ times. Warriors with $0$ HP do not retreat until the battle finishes, so warriors whose HP became $0$ from turret attacks are still counted in $a$ and $b$.
3. After the battle finishes, warriors with $0$ HP retreat and leave the battlefield. Buildings with $0$ HP are destroyed.

The rule of each attack is as follows:

- Among the opponent's warriors with positive HP, decrease the HP of the warrior with minimum HP by 1. If multiple such warriors exist, the warrior with the lowest number is targeted.
- If there is no opponent's warrior with positive HP, and the opponent's building has positive HP, the HP of the opponent's building is decreased by 1.

### 4 | Game End

During $200$ days from the game start, if the Headquarter is destroyed, then the player whose Headquarter is not destroyed wins.

If two headquarters are destroyed on the same day, or both headquarters are not destroyed in $200$ days, then the side whose HQ has more HP wins.

If the HP of two HQ are equal, then the game ends in a draw.

### 5 | Detailed Rules: Battlefield Generation Method

Since the structure of the battlefield is given as input, you do not need to know the generation rules to solve the problem.

If you would like to know how the battlefield is generated, the details are as follows:

<Details summary="Details">

### Battlefield

The battlefield can be represented as an infinitely sized Euclidean plane.

- The battlefield has exactly $N = 2N'+1$ polygonal areas.
- Each area has a **center**, which is a point inside the polygon excluding the border.
- Two areas are adjacent if they share at least one side.
- Each player's **headquarter** is established in the leftmost area #$0$ and the rightmost area #$N-1$.

The battlefield contains special areas called strongholds.

- $K = 2K'+1$ areas are strongholds. Buildings can be constructed on strongholds.

The battlefield structure is randomly generated for every game. Specific rules can be found below.

#### Generation Process

In the description below, $L = 10^4$, $D = 100$, and $A = 24$.

Each trial is independent, and when selecting a target from a specific range, all targets within that range are selected with equal probability.

(1) Determine $N'$ and $K'$.

- $N'$ is randomly selected from integers between $25$ and $54$ inclusive.
- Subsequently, $K'$ is randomly selected from integers between $\left\lceil \frac{0.15(2N'+1)-1}{2} \right\rceil$ and $\left\lfloor \frac{0.2(2N'+1)-1}{2} \right\rfloor$ inclusive.

(2) In the Euclidean coordinate plane, let $S$ be a circle centered at the origin with radius $L$.

(3) Initially, $P = \{(0, 0)\}$. Repeat the following $N'$ times.

- Select a grid point on or inside the boundary of $S$ and set it as $u=(x_u, y_u)$.
- If there exists a point $(x_v, y_v) = v \in P$ satisfying one of the following, select $u$ again. $\mathrm{dist}(u, v)$ is the Euclidean distance between point $u$ and $v$.
  - $x_u = x_v$
  - $\mathrm{dist}(u, v) < D$
- Add $u$ and $u'$, which is the point reflection of $u$ across the origin $O$, to $P$.

After the repetition, $|P| = 2N' + 1$. Number the points from $0$ to $N-1$ in increasing order of the $x$ coordinates.

(4) Draw a circle $C$ centered at the origin $O$ with radius $1.5L$ and divide it into $A$ equal parts. Specifically, let $t_1$ and $t_2$ be the two points where $C$ intersects the $x$ axis. Find the points that divide the upper arc and the lower arc connecting $t_1$ and $t_2$ into $A/2$ equal parts, respectively. Let these points be $Q$.

(5) Calculate the Voronoi diagram and the Delaunay triangulation based on the points in $P \cup Q$. Collect the centroids of the Voronoi diagram polygons that are dual to each point in $P$ and call this $P'$, and reassign the numbers given to each point. Then, calculate the Voronoi diagram and the Delaunay triangulation based on $P' \cup Q$.

(6) The Voronoi diagram polygon that is dual to point $t$ becomes area #$t$, and point $t$ becomes the center of area #$t$. If the center of an area is point $u$, that area is adjacent to areas whose centers are points $v \in P$ connected to $u$ in the Delaunay triangulation. Simply put, each area is adjacent to the areas connected by the Delaunay triangulation that were not formed from $Q$.

(7) Initially, $R = \{N'\}$. Repeat the following $K'$ times.

- Choose a random $t$ such that $t \in [0, N-1]$ and $t \not \in R$.
- If for one or more $r \in R$, area #$t$ or area #$N-1-t$ is adjacent to area #$r$, draw $t$ again.
- If area #$t$ and area #$N-1-t$ are adjacent, draw $t$ again.
- Add $t$ and $N-1-t$ to $R$.

After the iteration, $|R| = 2K' + 1$. For each $r \in R$, area #$r$ is a stronghold.

</Details>

## Interaction

Your program must read input from standard input (`stdin`) and follow the protocol of each command.

#### Ready

```
READY (LEFT | RIGHT)
N K
x_{0} ... x_{N-1}
y_{0} ... y_{N-1}
p_{0} ... p_{K-1}
a_{0} b_{0,0} ... b_{0,a_{0}-1}
...
a_{N-1} b_{N-1,0} b_{N-1,1} ... b_{N-1,a_{N-1}-1}
```

This command informs the player of the side and the map of the battlefield.

- The first line indicates which side of Headquarter the player uses.
- The second line contains $N$ and $K$.
- $x_i$ and $y_i$ are the $x$ and $y$-coordinates of the center of area #$i$.
- $p_i$s are the indices of the strongholds.
- Area #$i$ is adjacent to $a_i$ areas. The indices of adjacent areas are given as $b_{i,j}$ ($0 \le j < a_i$).

Player must output `OK` in $1\,000$ms to indicate the program is ready. Failing to do so results in Time Limit Exceeded(TLE) and forfeits the game.

#### Command Start

```
START TURN T
```

At the start of each turn, the `START` command is given. $T$ denotes the day index.

#### Deliver Command

```
COMMAND
...
MOVE a d
TRAIN n
UPGRADE d
...
END
```

Player can start the command phase with the `COMMAND` command. Player can submit any number of commands in any order. Then, mark the end of the list with the `END` command.

The initial warriors are indexed `A1`, `A2`, and `A3` for the left player, and `B1`, `B2`, and `B3` for the right player.

- `MOVE`: Deliver a Move command to move warrior #$a$ to area #$d$.
- `TRAIN`: Train $n$ warriors at the headquarter. There must be at most $1$ such command in the command phase.
- `UPGRADE`: Upgrade or repair the allied building in area #$d$. If there is no building in area #$d$, build a base instead.

Player must submit the list within $100$ms after receiving the `START` command (that is, output the `END` command). If more than $100$ms is used, for the exceeded time $t$ms, $\displaystyle \left \lceil \frac{t}{100} \right \rceil$ countdown tokens are consumed. If time is exceeded with no remaining countdown tokens, the player loses by Time Limit Exceeded(TLE). Each player starts with $5$ countdown tokens.

If an invalid command is submitted, the player immediately loses by Wrong Answer(WA). If invalid commands are submitted at the same time, the game becomes a draw.

#### View Result

```
TURN T
TIME T_x R_x T_y R_y
UPGRADE N
t_1 d_1
...
TRAIN N
a_1 ... a_N
MOVE N
a_1 d_1
...
DAMAGE N
C_1 a_1 h_1
...
SIEGE N
t_1 d_1 h_1
...
```

If both players finish submitting commands normally, the result is returned. If $N = 0$ in a `TRAIN`, `UPGRADE`, `MOVE`, `DAMAGE`, or `SIEGE` section, no following line is given for that section.

- `TURN` $T$: Indicates that information for the current day $T$ is shown.
- `TIME` $T_x$ $R_x$ $T_y$ $R_y$:
  - $T_x$ and $T_y$ indicate how much time you and the opponent used this turn, respectively (ms).
  - $R_x$ and $R_y$ indicate how many countdown tokens remain for you and the opponent, respectively.
- `UPGRADE` $N$:
  - $N$, the number of buildings built, upgraded, or repaired this turn, is given.
  - The next $N$ lines give the $t_i$, `A` or `B`, the owner of the building and $d_i$, the index of area of the building in order.
- `TRAIN` $N$:
  - $N$, the number of warriors trained this turn, is given.
  - The next line gives the indices of newly trained warriors in order. Warrior indices are assigned uniquely, increasing sequentially from 1 for each player.
  - Warrior indices are always in the form of `A` or `B` followed by an integer (e.g. `A12`, `B7`). If it starts with `A`, it is a warrior of the left player; if it starts with `B`, it is a warrior of the right player.
- `MOVE` $N$:
  - $N$, the number of warriors moved this turn, is given.
  - The next $N$ lines give the index $a_i$ of the moved warrior and the index $d_i$ of the area it moved to in order.
- `DAMAGE` $N$:
  - $N$, the number of damage records for warriors this turn, is given.
  - The next $N$ lines give the damage cause $C_i$, the index $a_i$ of the damaged warrior, and the damage amount $h_i$ in order.
  - $C_i$ is one of the following: `TURRET`(damage by building turret), `COMBAT`(damage by combat between warriors), `HUNGER`(damage by lack of food cost in Supply Step).
- `SIEGE` $N$:
  - $N$, the number of damage records for buildings this turn, is given.
  - The next $N$ lines give the building owner $t_i$ ($t_i$ is `A` or `B`), area #$d_i$ of the damaged building, and the siege damage amount $h_i$ in order.

The result of each turn ends with the `END` command.

### Game End

```
FINISH
```

When the game ends for any reason, the `FINISH` command is given. **When this command is received, the program must immediately terminate gracefully regardless of the game state.** In other words, if the `FINISH` command is given instead of the result after a turn ends, the program must immediately terminate before trying to read the result.
