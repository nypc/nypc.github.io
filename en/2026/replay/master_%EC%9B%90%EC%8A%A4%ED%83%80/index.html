2.2.5 Models That Didn't Pan Out

  • mcts-agent.py: tried applying MCTS early on, but it didn't apply well. Presumably the space-explosion problem of needing belief states because turns are simultaneous. Settled for the weak application later in MCCCTS.
  • The HRL family: meta-HRL, CAU (Commander-and-Unit, an HRL structure in the meta family),

CCCAU (an HRL structure in the CCC family), and CCC-SGTS-HRL all either didn't actually have an HRL structure, or collapsed against the existing single MLP. It could have been a problem with the training method, or with the structure itself. Either way, it was my ambitious project, and it ended unrealized.

  • As an aside, even after I'd written out the HRL structure plan, the LLM frequently reduced the heads on its own or collapsed it into a single MLP. That was the most annoying part.
  • PPoppoPN: Phase Policy + opponent action recognition + Positive-Negative state recognition (nickname: 뽀뽀뽕)
  • PP — separate models per sub-phase, each learning only that phase's management.
  • oppo — recognizes the opponent's strategy by comparing their actions against our own strategies. But if the opponent's actions belong to a strategy not in our repertoire, it's hard to infer, and the training pool was small, making it hard to learn.
  • PN — effectively a device devised as a value function; rewards were sparse (sparse reward), so it was hard to learn.
    • Also, even when currently in a winning state, if the actual game is lost on a comeback it learns the entire process as a mistake — that's the problem. So there should have been some explicit metric.
  • Teacher model: tried to build the strongest possible model to train from, going as far as state-of-the-art models like transformers, but the heuristic pool wasn't diverse enough to make it the strongest.

2.4 star: The abcd-priority-agent Model

A phased strategy model organized in the order A > B > C > D

2.4.1 The Problem

Rushes of 333–555 warriors became popular: they target the early game, when the starting 500500500 gold has to go into base construction, by instead spending the 500500500 gold on warriors and attacking HQ.

Early on it was advantageous to send 222 warriors to strongholds to claim bases first. Because of that, HQ often had only 111 warrior, and was vulnerable to 333–555-warrior rushes.

2.4.1.1 Attempts at a Solution

We tried recalling the warriors sent out to expand when enemy warriors came near HQ. Depending on the distances between the map's strongholds, the warriors often returned too late and we lost, or we started out at a labor disadvantage.

For a 555-warrior rush, they'd spent their early 500500500 gold, so if we could reliably hold, we won. For 333–555-warrior rushes, defending at HQ with (attackers−1)(\text{attackers} - 1)(attackers−1) was also possible.

The cause of losing to 333–555-warrior rushes was the warrior deficit from the gold and personnel spent on base construction. The gold and personnel spent building the 222nd base in particular were fatal. So we sent only 111 warrior to build the 111st base and had it watch the opponent's behavior.

If the opponent also built their 111st base, they spent gold and labor, so it was hard for them to mass warriors. And even if they massed warriors after that, our per-turn income had grown, making the rush easier to hold.

2.4.1.2 What Was Implemented

Phase A

Send only 111 warrior to the nearest stronghold and build the first base. Once the enemy has built their first base, move to B. When enemy warriors are within 222 areas of HQ, train so that (attackers within 2 areas−1)(\text{attackers within 2 areas} - 1)(attackers within 2 areas−1) are at HQ. If HQ has no labor, train to fill the labor slots.

Phase B

After confirming the enemy's first construction, start building the 222nd base. Move to C.

2.4.2 Mid-Game Structure (C–D)

At game start, count the empty strongholds on the battlefield excluding the central stronghold. Set half the empty strongholds excluding the center−1=optimal base count\text{half the empty strongholds excluding the center} - 1 = \text{optimal base count}half the empty strongholds excluding the center−1=optimal base count.

Phase C (establishing a hub)

The base located at the center of our HQ/bases is designated the "hub".

All trained warriors other than labor gather at the hub.

Harassment and defense are carried out around the hub's troops.

  • Harass if we have 444 or more warriors than the enemy, excluding both sides' labor and expansion personnel.

Phase D (reinforcing staying power)

Upgrade HQ when its level is equal to or lower than the enemy's.

When HQ's level is higher than the enemy's, or all upgrades are done,

  • Priority 111: upgrade the central-stronghold hub first.
  • Priority 222: upgrade the places most often passed through when moving from HQ.
  • Priority 333: upgrade bases within 3 areas of the hub.

2.4.3 Ideas

2.4.3.1 Relay (Boosting) System

When moving, if the route passes through one of our bases on the way to the destination, move the warrior to that intermediate base instead of the original destination. When the sent warrior enters the intermediate base, move the warrior that was already there on to the original destination.

This way personnel leave and arrive in the same turn, preserving labor while moving 111 turn faster.

2.4.3.2 HQ Relay (Boosting) System

On a turn when HQ is about to train troops, if there is an order for the trained troops, an HQ laborer acts instead. The troops produced at HQ then fill that labor slot.

2.4.3.3 Observation-Waypoint Defense System

When the enemy has 222 adjacent attack candidates and reinforcing after the attack path is confirmed would be too late, move the reinforcements to an intermediate "observation waypoint" off the enemy's route. Once the enemy's actual attack direction is confirmed on the next turn, reinforce that base.

2.4.3.4 Troop Preservation System

When there are no reinforcements against an enemy attack, check whether the base would be destroyed within 222 turns. If destruction within 222 turns is certain, on the turn the base is attacked, move all of its troops to the hub to preserve them.

2.4.3.5 Escort System

  1. A large enemy force of 888 or more is confirmed to be attacking our HQ directly
  2. An enemy base is built within 222 areas of HQ / an enemy stronghold is built on the path along which troops are sent to the hub
  3. Enemy troops are within 333 areas of HQ and defeat is expected

Reserve troops are stockpiled at HQ. These troops are called the "escort".

They reinforce our strongholds near HQ / assault enemy strongholds. They are released once the threat is gone.
